02

Salt is the whole problem here

Closer to open water than anywhere else on the run, and paint knows it.

Onshore wind carries salt off the water every day of the year, and it settles on whatever is parked in it. It does not need a wave to reach your car. Paint left bare in that air goes flat far faster than the same car would three streets inland, and chrome, badges and wheel faces show it before the panels do.

Interiors take a different beating. Sand travels in on feet and towels and works its way into carpet backing, and salt water plus sunscreen on seats is a combination that sets rather than wipes off. Seat and carpet extraction is the fix for most of that, and it is an add-on rather than standard because it is genuinely slow work.

Sealed and coated paint sheds salt-laden water instead of holding it, which is the entire argument for a coating this close to the beach, and why correction comes first.

Questions

Do you actually come to Mission Beach?

Yes. Mission Beach, Wongaling Beach, South Mission Beach, Bingil Bay and Garners Beach are all on the southern run, along with Tully, El Arish and Silkwood.

What is the travel charge?

A flat $25 for the southern run, shown in the quote before you book rather than added afterwards.

Can you do a holiday rental between guests?

Yes, and it is quoted on inspection rather than off the standard list, because turnaround cars vary far more than a private car does.

Is a coating worth it this close to the water?

Arguably more so than anywhere else on the coast. Salt water that sheets off a coated panel leaves far less behind when it dries, and that residue is what dulls paint here.

Can you get sand out of the carpet properly?

Seat and carpet extraction is the answer, because sand sits in the backing rather than on the surface where a vacuum can reach it.
